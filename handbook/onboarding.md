# Onboarding

Goal: by the end of week two, a new person has shipped something real, knows
how decisions get made here, and knows who to ask about what.

Every new person gets a named **onboarding buddy** (not their manager) whose
job is to answer "dumb" questions — which are the most valuable questions,
because they reveal what these docs failed to explain.

## Before day 1 (owner: hiring manager)

- [ ] Accounts, hardware, and access provisioned — arriving to broken access
  is the worst first impression a company can make
- [ ] Buddy assigned; first-week calendar seeded (kickoff, buddy check-ins)
- [ ] A real, small, shippable first task picked and written down

## Week 1 — context

- [ ] Read, in order: the repo [README](../README.md) (the operating system),
  [RFD 0001](../rfd/0001/README.md), [decision-making](decision-making.md),
  [communication](communication.md)
- [ ] Skim the RFD index end to end — titles and states only. This is the
  company's decision history; knowing it exists beats memorizing it
- [ ] Read the last two release retrospectives in `releases/cycles/`
- [ ] Meet everyone (at current size) / your working circle (later): 30
  minutes each, their view of what's working and what isn't
- [ ] Do the first task; ship it, however small

## Week 2 — participation

- [ ] Follow one active RFD discussion; comment at least once
- [ ] Run or dry-run one [playbook](../playbooks/README.md); file the PR for
  whatever it gets wrong — new eyes find what veterans skip
- [ ] Sit in on the current release cycle's weekly scope check
- [ ] End-of-week-two review with manager: what's confusing, what's missing
  from these docs, what should your next month look like

## The standing rule

Onboarding's exit criterion is a PR: every new person must fix at least one
wrong, stale, or confusing thing in this repository during their first two
weeks. If they can't find one, the docs are perfect (they are not) or the
person didn't read them.
