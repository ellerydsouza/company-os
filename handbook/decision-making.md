# Decision-making

## The default: write it down

Decisions of consequence go through an [RFD](../rfd/README.md). If it would
take a meeting to decide, or you'd want the "why" findable in a year, it gets
an RFD. This is the single most important norm in the company.

## Sizing the decision

- **Reversible and cheap** ("two-way door"): the person doing the work
  decides, tells the affected people, moves on. No RFD, no meeting.
- **Hard to reverse, or affects others' work** : RFD, normal 3–5 day
  discussion window.
- **Existential** (pricing model, pivot, fundraise terms, key hire): RFD plus
  a synchronous discussion — writing first, meeting second. The meeting
  discusses the written doc; it doesn't replace it.

## Who decides

Every decision has exactly one owner — the RFD author by default, the
accountable role otherwise. Input is widely invited; the decision is not a
vote. Convergence means objections were heard and answered or explicitly
acknowledged, not that everyone agrees.

Deadlocks escalate to the relevant decision owner (CEO for company-wide
matters until otherwise delegated), who decides *in the RFD's PR* so the
tie-break is on the record.

## Disagree and commit

Once an RFD is published, the discussion is over until someone writes a
superseding RFD with new information or results. Relitigating published
decisions in chat is the failure mode this whole system exists to prevent —
the honored move is "I still disagree; I'm committing; here's what would
change my mind."

## Meetings

Meetings are for building context and resolving deadlocks, not for making
routine decisions. Any meeting that makes a decision ends with someone named
to record it — in an RFD if it's consequential, in the relevant doc otherwise.
A decision that isn't written down didn't happen.
